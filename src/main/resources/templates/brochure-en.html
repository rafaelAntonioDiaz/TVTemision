PIML: CORRELATING THIN‑SECTION, CORE, AND LOG DATA

Our methodology integrates three sources of historical data to train models that do not hallucinate:

  • Thin‑section point‑counts: we extract the coordinates of mineralogical counting points and link them to diagenesis.
  • Core laboratory results: porosity, permeability, capillary pressure, and saturations.
  • Well logs: GR, RHOB, NPHI, resistivity, among others.

With these data, we train a Physics Informed model that estimates petrophysical properties without losing physical coherence. The result: an updated static reservoir model, ready to re‑evaluate reserves without drilling new wells.

TOTAL TRANSPARENCY: WE ARE NOT A BLACK BOX

In the oil industry, every reserve decision must be auditable. That is why we incorporate TreeSHAP, an explainability tool based on game theory that decomposes each prediction into the exact contribution of each input variable.

What does this mean for the operator?

  • Traceability: you can see how each piece of data (the thin section, the Gamma Ray log, the core porosity) influenced the estimated HFU.
  • Geoscientific validation: a petrophysicist can confirm that the learned relationships are geologically coherent, without spurious correlations.
  • Regulatory confidence: when presenting a reserve re‑evaluation report, you can demonstrate exactly the reasoning line of the model, eliminating the opacity of black boxes.

TreeSHAP allows us to guarantee that the model is not taking statistical shortcuts, but learning the true physical relationships that govern the reservoir. The result: accurate, explainable, and auditable predictions.

FROM HISTORICAL DATA TO AN UPDATED MODEL

The solution integrates thin‑section point‑counts, well logs, and core data to train Physics Informed Machine Learning models that estimate petrophysical properties and support the updating of the static reservoir model.
